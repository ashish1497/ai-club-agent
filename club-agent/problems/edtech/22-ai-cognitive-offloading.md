# 22. When does AI make students think less?

## The open question

When does handing recall and reasoning to AI weaken long-term skill, and can task design reverse the effect?

## Why it matters

Students now use AI for most coursework.

## Who cares

- School leaders and board-exam teachers setting AI-use policy
- Edtech designers building AI study tools
- Education research funders and evaluators

## In scope

- Defining which kinds of offloading (recall, problem-solving, writing) are being studied and over what time span
- Reviewing experimental evidence on AI use during practice and later unaided performance
- Specifying how an EEF-style causal design could be adapted to Indian secondary or board-exam students

## Out of scope

- Running studies on minors without parental consent and ethics approval
- Neuroscience or EEG data collection
- Recommending bans or rules to specific schools
- Building an AI tutoring product

## What is known

- In a field experiment with nearly 1,000 high-school maths students, unrestricted GPT-4 access improved practice grades by 48% but lowered later unaided exam grades by 17%, while a tutor with learning safeguards largely avoided the drop. ([Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning (PNAS, 2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/))
- Cognitive offloading is defined as using physical action or external tools to reduce the cognitive demand of a task. ([Cognitive Offloading, Risko and Gilbert (Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 2016)](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27542527))
- The EEF's 2026 call targets how generative AI use affects cognition and learning among 13-15 year-olds in England, asking which thinking processes are worth protecting from offloading and which AI could support. ([EEF Invitation to Tender: Impact of Generative AI on learner cognition (June 2026)](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/documents/tenders/Impact-generative-AI-learner-cognition_ITT_v.1.0.0.pdf))
- A small MIT EEG study (54 participants, preprint not peer reviewed) reported the weakest brain connectivity and lowest essay ownership among LLM-assisted writers. ([Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt (arXiv, 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872))

## What is unknown

- The conditions under which offloading helps or harms.

## Key terms

- **Cognitive offloading**: Handing part of a thinking task to a tool, such as a calculator, notes or an AI.
- **Desirable difficulty**: Effort during practice that feels harder but improves long-term memory and skill.
- **Cognitive load**: How much working memory a task demands at once.
- **Transfer / unaided performance**: How well a student does later on their own, without the tool.
- **Guardrails**: Design limits on an AI tool, such as giving hints instead of full answers.

## Possible angles

- **Theory:** desirable difficulty and cognitive load
- **Practice (no lab):** adapt the EEF study design to Indian board-exam students
