# 21. Do AI tutors produce learning that lasts?

## The open question

Do AI-tutor gains persist after 6–12 months, and for which learners, languages and schools in India?

## Why it matters

Large investments in AI tutoring assume lasting learning gains.

## Who cares

- Edtech product and learning-science leads
- State education departments and school-system administrators
- Philanthropic and impact funders backing AI tutoring

## In scope

- Defining what 'lasting' learning means and how it would be measured 6-12 months after an AI-tutor intervention
- Reviewing published evidence on persistence and fade-out of education interventions
- Specifying which learner, language and school subgroups in India matter for the question
- Designing (not running) a pre-registered study with an edtech partner

## Out of scope

- Building an AI tutor product
- Collecting children's data without verifiable parental consent and ethics approval
- Endorsing or rating specific commercial products
- Policy advice to government on procurement

## What is known

- A Harvard randomised trial found college physics students learned significantly more in less time with a custom AI tutor than in an in-class active-learning session. ([AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: an RCT (Scientific Reports, 2025)](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40537565))
- In Delhi, a personalised technology-aided after-school programme raised middle-school maths scores by 0.37 SD and Hindi by 0.23 SD over about 4.5 months. ([Disrupting Education? Experimental Evidence on Technology-Aided Instruction in India (AER, 2019)](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20171112))
- A review of education interventions concludes that fade-out of effects is widespread, often co-exists with persistence, and is not merely a measurement artefact. ([Persistence and Fade-Out of Educational-Intervention Effects (Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7787577/))
- J-PAL's Project AI Evidence first round funded eight studies, including Pratham's AI tool for Teaching at the Right Level in India. ([MIT News: New J-PAL research and policy initiative to test and scale AI innovations to fight poverty (Feb 2026)](https://news.mit.edu/2026/new-j-pal-research-policy-initiative-to-test-scale-ai-innovations-fight-poverty-0212))

## What is unknown

- Persistence of gains.
- Separating novelty effects from real learning.

## Key terms

- **Fade-out**: When a measured learning gain shrinks or disappears in later tests.
- **Novelty effect**: A temporary boost because a tool is new and exciting, not because it teaches better.
- **Effect size (SD)**: How big a gain is, expressed in standard deviations of test scores so studies can be compared.
- **Pre-registration**: Publicly recording the study design and outcomes before seeing the data.
- **Heterogeneous effects**: Differences in impact across groups such as language, grade or school type.
